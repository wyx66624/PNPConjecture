# Verifier-Invariant Obstruction Theory (VIOT)

## 1. Motivation

Raw configuration graphs, witness sets, and verification orders are presentations of computations rather than canonical objects attached to languages. Certificate padding, state renaming, bounded stuttering, dummy branching, and independent-check reordering can alter their combinatorics without changing which inputs are accepted.

A valid topological or categorical lower bound must therefore be defined only after these presentation artifacts have been identified, while retaining enough resource information to distinguish efficient from inefficient computation.

VIOT is a proposed higher-structural research program, not a completed proof of P versus NP.

## 2. Presentation category and resource-sensitive localization

A **verifier presentation** is a tuple
\[
\mathcal V=(X,W,V,t),
\]
where \(V:X\times W\to\{0,1\}\) is computable within a stated resource bound. Its recognized language is
\[
L(\mathcal V)=\{x:\exists w,\;V(x,w)=1\}.
\]

Elementary presentation moves should include:

1. polynomial-time bijective recoding of inputs or certificates;
2. addition or deletion of ignored nonempty certificate factors;
3. polynomially bounded stuttering of deterministic steps;
4. polynomial-size acyclic dummy branching and reconvergence;
5. replacement by a polynomial-time bidirectionally simulated verifier; and
6. reordering of independent checks with a certified dependency relation.

Let \(\mathsf{Ver}\) be a category of verifier presentations and resource-respecting simulations, and let \(S\) be the class generated by the elementary moves. The candidate semantic domain is a localization
\[
\mathsf{Ver}[S^{-1}],
\]
or a homotopy-coherent localization retaining higher simulations and quantitative overhead.

The already formalized quotient by equality of recognized languages is intentionally too coarse: it removes presentation artifacts but also forgets computational cost. It is a calibration object. The desired VIOT localization must lie between raw syntax and pure language semantics.

**Proof obligation VIOT-1.** Construct this resource-sensitive localization and prove that recognized language factors through it while polynomial overhead composes.

**Proof obligation VIOT-2.** Prove that every proposed obstruction descends to the localization rather than depending on a representative presentation.

## 3. Restriction site

For a Boolean function \(f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}\), let \(\mathcal R_n\) be the poset of partial assignments ordered by extension. A family of restrictions covering all total assignments supplies a Grothendieck-style coverage.

For a candidate circuit \(C\), possible local defect data over \(\rho\in\mathcal R_n\) include:

- counterexample sets
  \[
  D_{f,C}(\rho)=\{x\succeq\rho:f(x)\ne C(x)\};
  \]
- local refuter algorithms;
- certificates that a gate simplification is sound;
- proof-complexity witnesses for the restricted formula;
- equivariant sign data associated with complementary restrictions; and
- coherent families of partial circuit descriptions.

Restriction maps, variance, gluing, and resource bounds must be explicit. Calling an assignment a “sheaf” does not establish the sheaf axioms or a complexity consequence.

## 4. Derived obstruction and its required orientation

Choose a coefficient object \(A\) and derive an obstruction
\[
\Omega_k(f,C)\in H^k(\mathcal R_n;\mathcal D_{f,C})
\]
or an equivariant index in a suitable \(\mathbb Z_2\)-space.

The required logical orientation is:

\[
C=f\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Omega_k(f,C)=0.
\]

Therefore, if
\[
\forall C\in\mathrm{SIZE}(s(n)),
\qquad \Omega_k(f,C)\ne0,
\]
then
\[
f\notin\mathrm{SIZE}(s(n)).
\]

The abstract implication is formalized in `ObstructionSoundness.lean`. The hard work is to construct an obstruction for which the vanishing theorem, nonvanishing theorem, quantitative circuit-size bridge, and efficient explicitness are all independently proved.

A definition of \(\Omega\) that simply says “zero when a circuit exists and nonzero otherwise” is circular: evaluating its nonvanishing is exactly the original lower-bound problem. VIOT progress must therefore be measured by new computable or structurally tractable intermediate lemmas, not by a tautological reformulation.

## 5. Connection to current lower-bound methods

### Convergent gate elimination

Convergent rewriting can provide canonical local normal forms over bases where confluence holds. Such normal forms are candidates for local sections of a defect object. Failure of confluence over \(\mathcal B_2\) suggests retaining the full rewriting groupoid rather than selecting one normal form. Higher cells would encode critical-pair resolutions.

### Constructive refuters and range avoidance

A constructive lower bound returns, for every candidate circuit, an explicit input on which it fails. Local refuters over restrictions can be interpreted as sections. Cohomological data may measure the obstruction to choosing these sections coherently and efficiently.

### Proof complexity

A local refuter or defect certificate can be encoded as a propositional proof object. A transfer theorem could connect obstruction nonvanishing to lower bounds in a proof system. Conversely, bounded-arithmetic analysis can reveal when the invariant merely restates an unproved reflection principle.

### Equivariant topology and sign-rank

The 2026 sign-complex framework illustrates the required pattern: define a canonical \(\mathbb Z_2\)-space, prove that its equivariant index lower-bounds a recognized complexity measure, and compute the index for an explicit family. VIOT seeks an analogous theorem for circuit size, not an analogy-only argument.

### Restricted-model calibration

Recent threshold, monotone, and noncommutative arithmetic lower bounds should be used as calibration targets. VIOT should first reproduce a known lower bound in a model where a bridge theorem already exists. Failure at that stage would refute or sharply narrow a proposed obstruction before it is promoted to unrestricted circuits.

## 6. Formalized presentation no-go theorems

### 6.1 Certificate-product theorem

For input \(x\), define the accepting-certificate fiber
\[
W_x(V)=\{w:V(x,w)=1\}.
\]
For a dummy type \(D\), define
\[
V_D(x,(d,w))=V(x,w).
\]
The repository formalizes an explicit equivalence
\[
W_x(V_D)\cong D\times W_x(V).
\]
No nonemptiness assumption is needed for this structural equivalence. If \(D\ne\varnothing\), then
\[
L(V_D)=L(V).
\]

Consequently, any proposed language invariant that changes under an ignored nonempty certificate factor does not factor through the recognized language.

The `Unit`/`Bool` example gives a concrete witness: the original accepting fiber is a subsingleton, while the padded fiber contains the distinct witnesses `(false, ())` and `(true, ())`; the recognized language is unchanged.

Lean: `VerifierPadding.lean` and `WitnessStructureCounterexample.lean`.

### 6.2 Coarse semantic quotient

The repository formalizes same-language equivalence as a setoid on verifier presentations and forms the corresponding quotient. In this quotient, nonempty dummy padding is literal equality, and every quotient-defined invariant automatically ignores padding.

Lean: `VerifierSemanticQuotient.lean`.

This quotient is not yet resource-sensitive and cannot by itself encode a lower bound.

### 6.3 Constant-time diamond theorem

Consider two presentations of the universal language on the sole input `Unit`.

The direct presentation is
\[
s\longrightarrow t.
\]

The dummy branching presentation is
\[
\begin{array}{c}
&\ell&\\[-2mm]
s&&t\\[-2mm]
&r&
\end{array}
\qquad
s\to\ell\to t,
\quad
s\to r\to t.
\]

Every directed edge strictly increases a time rank, so both presentations are acyclic and have constant-depth accepting computations. They recognize exactly the same language. Nevertheless, after forgetting orientation, the direct presentation has no induced four-cycle, while the diamond has the chordless square
\[
s-\ell-t-r-s.
\]

Therefore raw trace-cycle structure is not a language invariant even under a constant-time acyclic dummy transformation.

Lean: `DiamondTraceNoGo.lean`.

This strengthens the padding warning: the problem is not limited to disconnected or nonterminating gadgets. Bounded dummy branching alone can change cycle structure.

## 7. General gadget-insertion target

The next no-go theorem should generalize the diamond construction:

> For every finite, polynomially navigable acyclic gadget \(G\) with a designated source and sink, and every verifier \(\mathcal V\), construct a verifier \(G\star\mathcal V\) that traverses a copy of \(G\) before simulating \(\mathcal V\), recognizes the same language, and incurs only polynomial overhead.

A stronger realization theorem would characterize which homotopy types can be introduced into the chosen trace complex by resource-bounded dummy structure. Such a theorem would not prove P versus NP; it would specify the quotient that any valid trace-based obstruction must survive.

## 8. Barrier audit

A successful VIOT lower bound must answer all of the following.

- **Relativization:** Which decisive construction fails or changes essentially in the presence of arbitrary oracle gates?
- **Natural proofs:** Is nonvanishing efficiently recognizable from a truth table, and is the property large? If both hold, how is the Razborov–Rudich barrier avoided?
- **Algebrization:** Does the argument survive low-degree algebraic extension of oracle data? If so, why is it not covered by algebrization?
- **Uniformity:** Is the obstruction family generated uniformly inside NP or another explicitly stated class?
- **Magnitude:** Does the index grow superpolynomially in a quantity proved to lower-bound circuit size, rather than merely linearly in the input length?
- **Model transfer:** Does a result for monotone, threshold, arithmetic, communication, query, or proof-complexity models transfer to ordinary Boolean circuits with controlled overhead?
- **Presentation invariance:** Is the invariant unchanged under all resource-bounded dummy operations already known to preserve the language?

## 9. Active conjectures

### VIOT Conjecture A: resource-sensitive simulation localization

There exists an effective homotopy-coherent localization of polynomial-time verifier presentations under bidirectional polynomial simulation, padding, bounded stuttering, recoding, and finite dummy branching. The localization retains a quantitative resource filtration and the recognized-language map factors through it.

Status: **CONJECTURE**.

### VIOT Conjecture B: calibrated recovery

A derived invariant on this localization recovers a known lower bound in at least one calibrated model, such as sign-rank, communication complexity, formula size, monotone circuits, or a restricted gate-elimination system.

Status: **OPEN GAP / first calibration target**.

### VIOT Conjecture C: critical-pair obstruction

For unrestricted binary circuits, the higher groupoid of simplification sequences carries a presentation-invariant equivariant obstruction whose quantitative index lower-bounds a nontrivial circuit resource.

Status: **CONJECTURE**. No direct circuit-size theorem is presently proved.

### VIOT Conjecture D: explicit NP target growth

For an explicit NP-complete family encoded on a presentation-invariant restriction site, the obstruction index exceeds every polynomial for all exactly computing circuit families.

Status: **OPEN GAP / central target**. This is essentially as strong as the desired circuit lower bound and must not be treated as established merely by naming the invariant.

## 10. Near-term theorem sequence

1. Formalize finite transition systems, polynomially bounded simulations, recoding, stuttering, and dummy branching.
2. Generalize the constant-time diamond theorem to finite acyclic source-to-sink gadgets.
3. Define the resource filtration and prove composition laws for simulation overhead.
4. Define the restriction category and defect presheaf for finite Boolean functions.
5. Prove functoriality under restrictions and descent under presentation equivalence.
6. Recover a known restricted lower bound as a calibration theorem.
7. Connect convergent gate-elimination refuters to local sections.
8. Retain the full higher rewriting groupoid when confluence fails over \(\mathcal B_2\).
9. Prove a quantitative circuit-soundness theorem.
10. Only after these steps, attempt target nonvanishing for SAT or another explicit NP-complete family.

A route remains active until one of its precise statements is proved or receives a counterexample or incompatibility theorem. The absence of a counterexample is not evidence that the conjecture is true.
