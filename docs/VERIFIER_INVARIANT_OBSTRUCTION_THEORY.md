# Verifier-Invariant Obstruction Theory (VIOT)

## 1. Motivation

Raw configuration graphs are presentations of computations, not canonical objects attached to languages. Certificate padding, state renaming, stuttering, dummy branches, and independent-check reorderings can alter trace topology without changing which inputs are accepted. A valid topological lower bound must therefore live after these presentation artifacts have been identified.

VIOT is a proposed higher-structural program, not a completed P-versus-NP proof.

## 2. Presentation category and localization

A **verifier presentation** is a tuple
\[
\mathcal V=(X,W,V,t),
\]
where \(V:X\times W\to\{0,1\}\) is computable within the stated resource bound. Its recognized language is
\[
L(\mathcal V)=\{x:\exists w,\;V(x,w)=1\}.
\]

Define elementary presentation moves:

1. polynomial-time bijective recoding of inputs or certificates;
2. addition or deletion of ignored nonempty certificate factors;
3. polynomially bounded stuttering of deterministic steps;
4. replacement by a polynomial-time bidirectionally simulated verifier;
5. reordering of independent checks with a certified dependency relation.

Let \(\mathsf{Ver}\) be a category of presentations and resource-respecting simulations. Let \(S\) be the class generated by the elementary moves. The first candidate object is a localization
\[
\mathsf{Ver}[S^{-1}],
\]
or, when strict localization is inconvenient, an \(\infty\)-categorical/homotopy quotient retaining coherent higher simulations.

**Proof obligation VIOT-1.** Construct the category and prove that \(L\) factors through the chosen equivalence while resource bounds are controlled polynomially.

## 3. Restriction site

For a Boolean function \(f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}\), let \(\mathcal R_n\) be the poset of partial assignments, ordered by extension. A family of restrictions that covers all total assignments defines a Grothendieck-style coverage.

For a candidate circuit \(C\), define local defect data over a restriction \(\rho\). Several variants are possible:

- counterexample sets \(D_{f,C}(\rho)=\{x\succeq\rho:f(x)\ne C(x)\}\);
- local refuter algorithms;
- certificates that a gate simplification is sound;
- proof-complexity witnesses for the restricted formula;
- equivariant sign data associated with pairs of complementary restrictions.

Restriction maps must be explicit and functorial. A presheaf is not enough merely because the word “sheaf” is used; all variance, gluing, and resource bounds must be proved.

## 4. Derived obstruction

Choose a coefficient object \(A\) and derive an obstruction
\[
\Omega_k(f,C)\in H^k(\mathcal R_n;\mathcal D_{f,C})
\]
or an equivariant index in a suitable \(\mathbb Z_2\)-space. The orientation is fixed by the following soundness schema.

### Obstruction soundness schema

For every candidate \(C\),
\[
C=f\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Omega_k(f,C)=0.
\]
Therefore, if one proves
\[
\forall C\in\mathrm{SIZE}(s(n)),\qquad \Omega_k(f,C)\ne0,
\]
then \(f\notin\mathrm{SIZE}(s(n))\).

This logical schema is formalized in `ObstructionSoundness.lean`. The mathematical work is to construct an obstruction for which both implications are nontrivial and efficiently specifiable.

## 5. Connection to current lower-bound methods

### Convergent gate elimination

Convergent rewriting can provide canonical local normal forms over bases where confluence holds. These normal forms are candidate sections of a local defect presheaf. Failure of confluence over \(\mathcal B_2\) is not ignored; it suggests retaining the entire rewriting groupoid rather than selecting one normal form. Higher cells record critical-pair resolutions.

### Constructive refuters and range avoidance

A constructive lower bound returns, for every candidate circuit, an explicit input on which it fails. Local refuters over restrictions can be viewed as sections. Cohomological data may measure the obstruction to choosing such sections coherently and efficiently across all restrictions.

### Proof complexity

A refuter or local defect certificate can be encoded as a propositional proof object. A transfer theorem could relate nonvanishing obstruction to lower bounds in a proof system. Conversely, bounded-arithmetic formalizability can expose whether the invariant is merely restating an unproved reflection principle.

### Equivariant topology and sign-rank

The 2026 sign-complex framework shows the required pattern: define a canonical \(\mathbb Z_2\)-space, prove its equivariant index lower-bounds a recognized complexity measure, then compute the index for an explicit family. VIOT seeks an analogous bridge for circuit size, not an analogy-only argument.

## 6. The verifier-padding no-go principle

The formalized padding theorem yields:

> Any purported language invariant that changes when a nonempty ignored certificate factor is added does not factor through the recognized language.

A stronger research target is the **gadget insertion theorem**:

> For any finite polynomially navigable directed gadget \(G\) with a source-to-sink path and any verifier \(\mathcal V\), construct an equivalent verifier \(G\star\mathcal V\) that traverses \(G\) before running \(\mathcal V\).

If raw trace homology detects \(G\), then arbitrary homology can be inserted into an easy language. Proving the exact theorem for each proposed trace complex is a required adversarial test.

## 7. Barrier audit

A successful VIOT lower bound must answer:

- **Relativization:** Which construction fails, or changes essentially, when arbitrary oracle gates are allowed?
- **Natural proofs:** Is nonvanishing efficiently recognizable from the truth table, and is the property large? If both hold, what cryptographic assumption is being challenged or avoided?
- **Algebrization:** Does the obstruction survive low-degree algebraic extension of oracle data? If so, why is it not covered by algebrization?
- **Uniformity:** Is the obstruction family generated uniformly inside NP or another explicitly stated class?
- **Magnitude:** Does the index grow superpolynomially in a measure that provably lower-bounds circuit size, rather than only linearly in the number of variables?

## 8. Initial conjectures

### VIOT Conjecture A: simulation localization

There exists a computable derived invariant of verifier presentations that is invariant under polynomial-time bidirectional simulation and whose restriction to a suitable communication matrix recovers the \(\mathbb Z_2\) sign-complex index.

Status: **CONJECTURE**.

### VIOT Conjecture B: critical-pair obstruction

For unrestricted binary circuits, the higher groupoid of simplification sequences has an equivariant obstruction whose quantitative index lower-bounds the number of nonlocal critical-pair resolutions required by the circuit.

Status: **CONJECTURE**. A direct relation to circuit size has not been proved.

### VIOT Conjecture C: explicit target growth

For an explicit NP-complete family encoded with a presentation-invariant restriction site, the obstruction index exceeds every polynomial for all exactly computing circuit families.

Status: **OPEN GAP / central target**. This statement is essentially as strong as the desired circuit lower bound and must not be treated as established merely by naming the invariant.

## 9. Near-term theorem sequence

1. Formalize verifier presentations, polynomial simulations, padding, stuttering, and recoding.
2. Prove gadget-insertion no-go theorems for raw path, order, and clique complexes.
3. Define the restriction category and defect presheaf for finite Boolean functions.
4. Prove functoriality under restrictions and presentation equivalence.
5. Recover a known restricted lower bound—such as sign-rank or a formula lower bound—as a calibration test.
6. Connect convergent gate-elimination refuters to local sections.
7. Search for a quantitative obstruction that remains meaningful when confluence fails over \(\mathcal B_2\).
8. Only then attempt an NP-versus-\(P/poly\) target.

The route remains active until one of its precise conjectures receives a counterexample or incompatibility theorem.
