# Formal audit of arXiv:2510.17829

## Scope

The audited manuscript defines a computation path as a sequence of configurations connected by valid directed computation steps. It then declares the free abelian group on such paths and defines the boundary by deleting each configuration with alternating sign. It also claims that a category of computational problems and reductions is additive, defines a verification-order functional \(\rho\), proposes a contracting homotopy for P computations, and derives \(P\ne NP\).

The following counterexamples target the exact displayed definitions. They do not assert that all future uses of homology in complexity theory are impossible.

## Theorem 1: the proposed boundary is not closed

Let the directed configuration graph have three vertices
\[
c_0\longrightarrow c_1\longrightarrow c_2
\]
and no edge \(c_0\to c_2\). Designate \(c_0\) as initial and \(c_2\) as terminal.

Then
\[
\pi=(c_0,c_1,c_2)
\]
is a valid length-two computation path. The proposed middle face is
\[
\pi^{(1)}=(c_0,c_2).
\]
It is not a valid computation path because \(c_0\to c_2\) is not a valid step. Hence the displayed formula
\[
d_2(\pi)=(c_1,c_2)-(c_0,c_2)+(c_0,c_1)
\]
does not land in the free abelian group generated by valid length-one paths.

**Conclusion.** Before one can ask whether \(d^2=0\), \(d\) must first be a map between the stated groups. It is not. The usual simplicial cancellation proof applies only to a collection closed under faces. Directed path homology repairs this by changing the allowed chains and/or projecting to a face-stable subcomplex; that repair is absent from the audited definition.

## Theorem 2: the claimed computational category is not additive

Use ordinary polynomial-time many-one reductions as morphisms. Let
\[
A=\Sigma^*,\qquad B=\varnothing
\]
over a nonempty input domain. A many-one reduction \(f:A\to B\) would have to satisfy
\[
x\in A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(x)\in B
\]
for every \(x\). The left side is true and the right side false, so no such \(f\) exists.

Thus the hom-set \(\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)\) is empty. A preadditive category requires every hom-set to be an abelian group, hence nonempty and equipped with a zero morphism. Moreover, the empty language cannot be a zero object: there is no morphism from every language to it.

The manuscript's phrase “run \(f\) and \(g\) in parallel and combine results” does not define a binary operation on reductions, does not specify an identity or inverse, and cannot repair an empty hom-set.

**Conclusion.** The asserted additive-category structure fails even before checking associativity, commutativity, or compatibility with composition.

## Theorem 3: the proposed verification-order average is not additive under composition

For an order \(\sigma=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_m)\), the manuscript defines
\[
\rho(\sigma)=\frac{1}{m-1}
\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_{k+1}-\sigma_k).
\]

Take
\[
a=(1,2),\qquad b=(3,4).
\]
Then
\[
\rho(a)=1,\qquad \rho(b)=1.
\]
Their concatenation is \(a\mathbin{\|}b=(1,2,3,4)\), all three adjacent differences are positive, and therefore
\[
\rho(a\mathbin{\|}b)=\frac{1+1+1}{3}=1\ne2=\rho(a)+\rho(b).
\]

The missing cross-boundary term and the changed normalization denominator make additivity generically false. Therefore the claimed identity used to prove \(\rho(d_2\sigma)=0\) is invalid.

## Theorem 4: the proposed contracting homotopy fails on one edge

Consider a deterministic computation consisting of the unique edge
\[
e=[c_0,c_1],
\]
where \(c_1\) is terminal. With the standard orientation,
\[
d(e)=c_1-c_0.
\]
The manuscript's rule gives
\[
s_0(c_0)=e,\qquad s_0(c_1)=0,\qquad s_1(e)=0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
(ds+sd)(e)=s_0(d e)=s_0(c_1)-s_0(c_0)=-e,
\]
not \(e\). At degree zero the unaugmented formula also gives
\[
d\,s_0(c_0)=c_1-c_0,
\]
not \(c_0\).

This is not a minor omitted case: the displayed homotopy formula has the wrong behavior on the smallest nontrivial deterministic computation. A corrected contraction would require a different complex and a carefully defined cone point, augmentation, or extra degeneracy compatible with all faces.

## Theorem 5: verifier-order topology is not automatically a language invariant

Let \(V(x,w)\) be any Boolean verifier and let \(D\) be any nonempty dummy certificate type. Define
\[
V_D(x,(d,w))=V(x,w).
\]
Then
\[
\exists(d,w)\;V_D(x,(d,w))=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists w\;V(x,w)=1.
\]
So \(V_D\) recognizes exactly the same language as \(V\), even though its certificate presentation and possible traces may have arbitrarily many dummy variants.

More elaborate polynomial-time padding can insert stuttering steps, reorder independent dummy checks, or route through a chosen finite gadget before running \(V\). Any topology extracted directly from one verifier's raw traces must therefore prove invariance under these transformations. Otherwise the topology measures implementation choices, not language complexity.

## Consequence for the claimed P-versus-NP proof

The claimed chain complex is not defined on the stated generators. Independently, the categorical, cocycle, and contraction lemmas used to derive the separation each have explicit counterexamples. Therefore the main theorem does not follow from the manuscript.

This is a rigorous refutation of that proof route in its published form. It is not a proof of \(P=NP\), nor does it rule out a repaired topological lower-bound framework. The repair requirements lead to VIOT.
